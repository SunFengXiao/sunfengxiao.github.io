Quantum Optics and Quantum Information

Non-Gaussian Quantum Entanglement Detection

Quantum-entanglement detection and classification in multimode non-Gaussian systems using quantum Fisher information and higher-order correlation operators.

>> View Publication

Remote Generation of Schrödinger Cat States

A theoretical entanglement-based scheme for preparing Schrödinger cat states, demonstrated in cavity-magnonic systems.

>> View Publication

Quantum entanglement is a core resource for quantum-information tasks. As the number of modes in continuous-variable quantum systems grows, entanglement structures become rapidly more complex, making efficient detection and classification difficult with conventional methods. Non-Gaussian features introduced by nonlinear interactions also render many common criteria insufficient. To address this challenge, we have developed quantum-entanglement detection methods based on quantum Fisher information and higher-order correlation matrices, applied them to light–matter coupled systems including cavity optomechanical and cavity-magnonic platforms, and collaborated on neural-network models for multimode non-Gaussian entanglement classification. We further proposed an entanglement-based scheme for the remote generation of Schrödinger cat states and collaborated on its experimental verification, demonstrating its potential for quantum metrology.

Nonreciprocal Amplification and Topological Edge States

Nonlocal nonreciprocal couplings implemented with optical fibers reveal the relationship between nonreciprocal amplification and topological edge states.

>> View Publication

Understanding light–atom coupling is fundamental to the study of light–matter interactions and provides a platform for simulating and controlling quantum and topological phase transitions. We investigate superradiant quantum phase transitions and multistability in representative light–atom coupled systems, including Dicke and Rabi models, together with lattice models featuring nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor interactions. Our work reveals tricritical phenomena in finite-temperature Fermi gases and their relationship to system dimensionality, and simulates quantum frustration in the J1–J2 model. We also introduce nonlocal couplings between lattice sites using optical fibers, establish a correspondence between nonreciprocal amplification and topological edge states, and reveal bulk modes with cluster-localization characteristics induced by nonlocal coupling.

Ultrafast Generation of Optical Cat States with Free Electrons

Quantum-path interference in strongly coupled free-electron–light interactions enables ultrafast generation of optical Schrödinger cat states.

>> View Publication

Traditional strong-field physics usually treats driving light as a classical field and neglects its quantum statistical effects. With advances in ultrafast laser technology, the role of quantum properties of the driving field in strong-field phenomena such as tunneling ionization and high-harmonic generation is becoming increasingly apparent. We first revealed the modulation of above-threshold ionization of hydrogen atoms by strongly squeezed coherent light, predicted photoelectron momentum spectra distinct from those driven classically, and systematically explained the roles of bunching and superbunching in ultrafast quantum optical fields. We also proposed a theoretical scheme for ultrafast generation of optical Schrödinger cat states using the modulation of ultrafast optical fields through free-electron interactions, opening a new route for ultrafast quantum-information processing and quantum metrology.
